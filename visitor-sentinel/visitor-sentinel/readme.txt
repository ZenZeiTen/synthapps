Visitor analytics with IP address tracking, page activity tracking and fraudulent click detection for paid ads and on-site ads.

== Description ==

Visitor Sentinel records who visits your site, what they do on each page, and whether the clicks you pay for come from people.

= IP address tracking =

* Records each visitor's IP address, country (from Cloudflare, or any geolocation service through a filter), browser, operating system and device type.
* Resolves the real client address behind Cloudflare, load balancers and reverse proxies. Forwarded-for headers are believed only from proxies you trust, so visitors cannot fake their address.
* Three storage modes: full address, masked address (203.0.113.0) or no address at all (a keyed hash only). Fraud detection and blocking work in every mode.

= Page activity tracking =

* Pageviews, sessions, entry and exit pages, referrers and UTM campaigns.
* Time on page, active (visible) time, scroll depth and interaction counts.
* Clicks on outbound links, file downloads and any element marked with `data-vsen-track`.
* A live view of who is on the site now, and a full activity timeline for every visitor.
* Works with full-page caching: the tracker reports through the REST API.

= Fraudulent click detection =

Every paid ad visit (Google Ads gclid/gbraid/wbraid, Microsoft msclkid, TikTok ttclid, LinkedIn, X, Snapchat, Reddit, Yandex, or a paid utm_medium) and every click on an ad unit on your pages gets a fraud score from 0 to 100. Sixteen rules feed the score, including:

* Bot, crawler, HTTP library and headless browser user agents, and `navigator.webdriver`.
* Clicks generated by script instead of a person.
* Paid landings the server saw but no browser ever ran (no JavaScript).
* An ad click ID reused from a different IP.
* Too many ad clicks from one IP per hour or per day.
* The same ad clicked again within 10 minutes.
* Ads clicked within a fraction of a second of page load, or with no mouse, touch, key or scroll activity first.
* Paid visitors who leave at once without interacting.
* Many visitor IDs from one IP (cleared cookies), rate-limit violations, missing browser headers, and IP ranges you mark as suspicious.

Clicks inside ad iframes such as AdSense are detected too.

= Protection =

* Automatic blocking at a score you choose, with expiry.
* Blocked IPs can be only recorded, shown your site without ads, or refused the site with a 403. Administrators and the login page are never blocked, and search and ad crawlers are never blocked automatically.
* Export the blocklist in Google Ads IP exclusion format.
* Hourly email digest of new fraudulent clicks.

= Privacy =

* Honors Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control (optional).
* Can wait for consent through the WP Consent API or a one-line call from any cookie banner.
* No third-party services, no cookies; returning visitors are recognized by a random ID in browser storage, which can be switched off.
* Automatic data retention and suggested privacy policy text.

== Installation ==

1. Upload the `visitor-sentinel` folder to `/wp-content/plugins/`, or upload the zip under Plugins > Add New.
2. Activate the plugin.
3. Open Visitor Sentinel > Settings. If your site is behind Cloudflare, tick "This site is behind Cloudflare". Behind another proxy, choose its header and enter its addresses.
4. List your ad units under "Ad elements", or wrap ad code in `[vsen_ad label="sidebar"]...[/vsen_ad]`.

== Frequently Asked Questions ==

= Does it work with page caching? =

Yes. The tracker reports through the REST API from the browser, and ad hiding for flagged visitors works on cached pages. The server-side "no JavaScript" check needs the uncached page, which most caches serve for URLs carrying ad click IDs.

= Does it block my ad clicks on Google Ads? =

No plugin can stop Google from charging for a click that already happened. Visitor Sentinel finds fraudulent clicks, hides ads from the sources, and exports their IPs in the format of the Google Ads campaign "IP exclusions" setting (up to 500 per campaign), so the same sources stop seeing your ads. Use the fraud report as evidence when you ask the ad network for a refund.

= How do I track custom events? =

Add `data-vsen-track="label"` to an element, or call `VisitorSentinel.track('label')` from your own JavaScript.

= Developer hooks =

* `vsen_country` filter: return a two-letter country code for an IP.
* `vsen_fraud_facts` and `vsen_fraud_result` filters: add your own signals or change a score.
* `vsen_fraud_detected` action: fires when a click first becomes fraudulent.
* `vsen_should_track`, `vsen_rate_limit`, `vsen_allowed_hosts`, `vsen_capability` filters.
* `vsen_blocked_request` action: fires before a blocked visitor is refused.
